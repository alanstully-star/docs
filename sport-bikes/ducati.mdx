Ducati is an Italian motorcycle maker based in Bologna. It started building motorcycles in the 1940s and is best known for its sport bikes, its racing success and its desmodromic valve system. Its bikes include some of the most successful superbikes in World Superbike and the dominant bikes of recent MotoGP seasons. Ducati is owned by Audi, part of the Volkswagen Group.

Ducati's faired superbikes are also listed on the [superbikes page](/sport-bikes/superbikes), and its racing bikes on the [MotoGP page](/sport-bikes/motogp).

## What makes a Ducati

- **Desmodromic valves.** Ducati opens and closes its engine valves mechanically instead of using return springs. The system is a Ducati trademark and is used across its road and racing engines.
- **V-twin and V4 engines.** Ducati built its name on the L-twin, a V-twin laid out in an L shape. Its newer sport bikes use V4 engines, and the Panigale V2 and Monster use V-twins.
- **Race-derived design.** Panigale engines, electronics and aerodynamics are developed with Ducati Corse, the racing division.
- **Italian styling.** Red is the signature color, and Ducati bikes are known for their sharp, distinctive design.

## Comparing the road bikes

| Bike | Type | Engine | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Panigale V4 | Superbike | V4, 1,103 cc | Experienced riders and track days |
| Panigale V2 | Superbike | V-twin | Riders stepping up to a Panigale |
| Streetfighter V4 | Naked | V4 | Experienced riders who want very high power without a fairing |
| Monster | Naked | V-twin | Newer riders, city and mixed road riding |
| Multistrada V4 | Sport-touring | V4 | Long rides with sporty handling |

## Past Ducati sport bikes

| Model | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| 851 and 888 | Early Ducati superbikes of the late 1980s and early 1990s that helped establish Ducati in World Superbike |
| 916 | Launched in 1994 and designed by Massimo Tamburini. One of the most admired motorcycle designs ever made |
| 996 and 998 | Developments of the 916 that kept the same look with larger engines |
| 999 | Introduced in 2003 with a different, more angular design |
| 1098 and 1198 | Launched from 2007, returning to a design closer to the 916 |
| 1199 Panigale | Launched in 2012. Its frame was built around the engine instead of a traditional trellis, and the Panigale name replaced the older numbered models |
| 1299 Panigale | The last big V-twin Panigale before the V4 arrived in 2018 |
| Desmosedici RR | A limited road-going copy of the MotoGP bike, sold in 2008 |

## Racing

Ducati races in both of the main road-racing championships.

- **World Superbike.** Carl Fogarty won four titles on Ducatis, in 1994, 1995, 1998 and 1999. Troy Bayliss won in 2001, 2006 and 2008. Álvaro Bautista won in 2022 and 2023 on the Panigale V4 R.
- **MotoGP.** Casey Stoner won the 2007 title on the Desmosedici. Ducati later won with Francesco Bagnaia in 2022 and 2023, and Marc Márquez won the 2025 title on a Ducati.

## Ducati vs KTM

Ducati and KTM both build sport bikes for road and track, but they are aimed at different riders.

| | Ducati | KTM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Country | Italy | Austria |
| Main sport bikes | Panigale, Streetfighter, Monster | RC, Duke and Super Duke |
| Engines | V-twin and V4 | Single, parallel twin and V-twin |
| Entry point | Monster and Panigale V2 | 125 to 390 Duke |
| Top naked bike | Streetfighter V4 | 1390 Super Duke R |
| Racing | World Superbike and MotoGP | MotoGP with the RC16 |

See the [KTM Duke page](/sport-bikes/ktm-duke) for the Duke range.
