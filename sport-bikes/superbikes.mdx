Superbikes are fully faired, roughly 1,000 cc road bikes. They are the closest thing to a race bike you can buy and are the base for the World Superbike Championship (WorldSBK).

## Superbikes

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Ducati Panigale V4" icon="motorcycle">
    **Italy. 1,103 cc V4.** Ducati's flagship superbike, launched in 2018. Its Desmosedici Stradale engine is derived from the Desmosedici MotoGP bike, and the race-homologated Panigale V4 R uses a smaller 998 cc version to meet WorldSBK rules. Álvaro Bautista won the 2022 and 2023 WorldSBK titles on it. The Streetfighter V4 is the naked version.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Yamaha YZF-R1" icon="motorcycle">
    **Japan. 998 cc inline four.** First sold in 1998, the R1 uses a crossplane crankshaft, a firing order taken from Yamaha's YZR-M1 MotoGP bike that gives it a distinctive sound. Toprak Razgatlıoğlu won the 2021 WorldSBK title on it. In Europe it has been sold only as a track bike (R1 RACE) since 2025, because Yamaha did not update it for Euro5+ emission rules.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Honda CBR1000RR-R Fireblade" icon="motorcycle">
    **Japan. 999 cc inline four.** Introduced in 2020 with an engine and aerodynamic winglets developed with Honda's racing division, HRC. The SP version adds semi-active Öhlins suspension. The Fireblade name dates back to the original CBR900RR of 1992, which set the template for light, powerful superbikes.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Kawasaki Ninja ZX-10R" icon="motorcycle">
    **Japan. 998 cc inline four.** Kawasaki's superbike and one of the most successful WorldSBK bikes. Tom Sykes won the 2013 title on it, and Jonathan Rea won six straight titles from 2015 to 2020. The ZX-10RR is a limited, higher-spec version built for racing homologation.
  </Card>

  <Card title="BMW S 1000 RR" icon="motorcycle">
    **Germany. 999 cc inline four.** BMW's first superbike, launched in 2009. It uses ShiftCam variable valve timing for strong power at both low and high revs. The M 1000 RR is its higher-spec version with carbon winglets, and Toprak Razgatlıoğlu won the 2024 WorldSBK title on it.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Aprilia RSV4" icon="motorcycle">
    **Italy. 1,099 cc V4.** Launched in 2009 with a compact 65-degree V4 known for its sound and its race-derived electronics. Max Biaggi won the WorldSBK title on it in 2010 and 2012, and Sylvain Guintoli in 2014. The Tuono V4 is the naked version.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Middleweight sport bikes

Middleweights are lighter and less powerful than superbikes, which makes them easier to use on the road.

| Bike | Engine | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kawasaki Ninja ZX-6R | 636 cc inline four | A traditional supersport bike with a high-revving four-cylinder engine. One of the few 600-class supersports still sold for the road. |
| Aprilia RS 660 | 659 cc parallel twin | A light sport bike whose engine is based on the front cylinders of the RSV4's V4. It has the same style of electronics as Aprilia's superbike. The Tuono 660 is the naked version. |
| Yamaha R7 | 689 cc parallel twin | Built around the engine from the MT-07 naked bike. It has a low weight and a manageable power delivery, and is popular for track days and beginner racing classes. |
| Triumph Daytona 660 | 660 cc inline triple | Launched in 2024 with the triple from the Trident 660. It is aimed at road riders who want sporty looks with comfortable, flexible power. |

## Entry-level sport bikes

These smaller bikes suit new riders and are often the first step toward bigger machines.

| Bike | Engine | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KTM RC 390 | 373 cc single cylinder | The faired version of the 390 Duke, first sold in 2014. KTM stopped selling it in all markets except India, with remaining stock sold through 2026. |
| Yamaha R3 | 321 cc parallel twin | A small sport bike styled like the R1 and R7, with a smooth twin that suits new riders. |
| Kawasaki Ninja 500 | 451 cc parallel twin | Replaced the Ninja 400 in 2024. A light, easy bike that is a common choice for first-time riders and entry-level racing. |
| Honda CBR500R | 471 cc parallel twin | Sold since 2013, it has a relaxed riding position and a reputation for reliability and low running costs. |

<Tip>
  If you are new to riding, start with an entry-level or middleweight bike and take a certified riding course before moving up to a superbike.
</Tip>

<Note>
  Model names, engine sizes and availability change by year and by country. Check the manufacturer's site for the current lineup in your market.
</Note>
