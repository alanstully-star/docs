The Duke is KTM's line of naked sport bikes: road bikes with no fairing, an upright riding position and the engine on show. The name goes back to the single-cylinder Duke of 1994, and the range now runs from small commuter-friendly bikes to some of the most powerful naked bikes on sale. KTM is an Austrian company, based in Mattighofen.

Dukes share their engines and many of their parts with KTM's faired sport bikes, such as the RC 390 on the [superbikes page](/sport-bikes/superbikes).

## The Duke range

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="390 Duke" icon="motorcycle">
    **Single cylinder, about 399 cc.** The entry point to the range, first sold in 2013. It is light and quick for its size, and popular with new riders who want a sporty bike. It is also the base for the faired RC 390.
  </Card>

  <Card title="790 / 890 Duke" icon="motorcycle">
    **Parallel twin, 799 cc and 889 cc.** The 790 Duke launched in 2018 and was nicknamed "The Scalpel". It was later developed into the 890 Duke, including the sportier 890 Duke R.
  </Card>

  <Card title="990 Duke" icon="motorcycle">
    **Parallel twin, 947 cc.** The updated middleweight Duke, introduced in 2024. It sits between the smaller twins and the V-twin Super Dukes.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Super Duke R" icon="motorcycle">
    **V-twin, over 1,300 cc.** The 1290 Super Duke R arrived in 2014 with the nickname "The Beast". It was followed by the 1390 Super Duke R, which uses a 1,350 cc V-twin. It is the top of the Duke range.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Comparing the Dukes

| Bike | Engine | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 390 Duke | Single cylinder, about 399 cc | New riders, city riding, light and cheap to run |
| 790 / 890 Duke | Parallel twin, 799 cc / 889 cc | Riders stepping up from a small bike, road and canyon riding |
| 990 Duke | Parallel twin, 947 cc | Experienced riders who want a strong middleweight |
| Super Duke R | V-twin, 1,301 cc / 1,350 cc | Experienced riders who want very high power on the road |

## Naked bike or sport bike?

A naked Duke has the same kind of engine and chassis as a sport bike but is more comfortable at everyday speeds. The riding position is more upright, which is easier on wrists and shoulders, and there is no fairing to protect you from wind at higher speeds. If you plan to ride on a track or at sustained high speeds, a faired bike from the [superbikes page](/sport-bikes/superbikes) is a better fit. For mixed road riding, many riders prefer a Duke.

<Note>
  Model names, engine sizes and availability change by year and by country. Check KTM's site for the current lineup in your market.
