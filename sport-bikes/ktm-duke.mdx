The Duke is KTM's line of naked sport bikes: road bikes with no fairing, an upright riding position and the engine on show. The name goes back to the single-cylinder 620 Duke of 1994, and the range now runs from small commuter-friendly bikes to some of the most powerful naked bikes on sale. KTM is an Austrian company, based in Mattighofen.

Dukes share their engines and many of their parts with KTM's faired sport bikes, such as the RC 390 on the [superbikes page](/sport-bikes/superbikes).

## Comparing the Dukes

| Bike | Engine | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 125, 200 and 250 Duke | Single cylinder | Learners and new riders |
| 390 Duke | Single cylinder, about 399 cc | New riders, city riding, light and cheap to run |
| 790 / 890 Duke | Parallel twin, 799 cc / 889 cc | Riders stepping up from a small bike, road and canyon riding |
| 990 Duke | Parallel twin, 947 cc | Experienced riders who want a strong middleweight |
| Super Duke R | V-twin, 1,301 cc / 1,350 cc | Experienced riders who want very high power on the road |

## Past Duke models

The Duke name has been used on several generations of bikes.

| Model | Engine | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 620 Duke | Single cylinder | The first Duke, launched in 1994 |
| 690 Duke | Single cylinder | A large, powerful single that was popular with riders who wanted a light, fast naked bike |
| 990 Super Duke | V-twin | The first Super Duke, introduced in 2005 and the ancestor of the 1290 and 1390 Super Duke R |
| 1290 Super Duke R | V-twin, 1,301 cc | Launched in 2014, nicknamed "The Beast". Replaced by the 1390 Super Duke R |
| 790 Duke | Parallel twin, 799 cc | Launched in 2018, nicknamed "The Scalpel". Developed into the 890 Duke |

## Key features of the Duke range

- **Naked design.** The engine, frame and suspension are visible, with an upright riding position.
- **Steel trellis frame.** Many Dukes use KTM's orange tubular frame, a signature look of the range.
- **Single, twin and V-twin engines.** The range starts with light, simple singles and moves up through parallel twins to the V-twin Super Dukes.
- **Electronics.** Larger Dukes typically have ride modes, traction control, cornering ABS and a color TFT display. The features vary by model and year.
- **Shared parts with the RC range.** Dukes and the faired RC bikes use similar engines and chassis, so choosing between them is mostly about riding position and bodywork.

## Duke vs RC

KTM sells many of its small and mid-size sport bikes in two forms: the naked Duke and the faired RC. The 390 Duke and the RC 390 are the best-known pair.

|  | Duke | RC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bodywork | None. A small headlight cowl and minimal plastics | Full fairing with a windscreen |
| Riding position | Upright, with a wide handlebar | Leaned forward, with clip-on handlebars |
| Best for | Daily riding, city streets, mixed roads | Track days and fast road riding |
| Wind protection | Little at higher speeds | Better, especially in a tuck |
| Comfort | More comfortable for short and long rides | Lower bars and a sportier position can tire wrists and shoulders |
| Availability | Sold in most markets | Limited. The RC 390 is now sold mainly in India, as covered on the [superbikes page](/sport-bikes/superbikes) |

KTM has also built larger faired sport bikes, such as the RC 8, and races the RC16 in [MotoGP](/sport-bikes/motogp).

## Naked bike or sport bike?

A naked Duke has the same kind of engine and chassis as a sport bike but is more comfortable at everyday speeds. The riding position is more upright, which is easier on wrists and shoulders, and there is no fairing to protect you from wind at higher speeds. If you plan to ride on a track or at sustained high speeds, a faired bike from the [superbikes page](/sport-bikes/superbikes) is a better fit. For mixed road riding, many riders prefer a Duke.
