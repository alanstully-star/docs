MotoGP is the top class of the FIM Road Racing World Championship Grand Prix. The championship began in 1949, making it the oldest motorsport world championship. Each race weekend also features the Moto2 and Moto3 support classes.

## History of the premier class

| Era | Engine rules |
| --- | --- |
| 1949 to 2001 | 500 cc class, dominated by two-stroke engines from the 1970s onward |
| 2002 to 2006 | Renamed MotoGP. Four-stroke engines up to 990 cc |
| 2007 to 2011 | Four-stroke engines up to 800 cc |
| 2012 to 2026 | Four-stroke engines up to 1,000 cc |
| 2027 onward | Four-cylinder, four-stroke engines up to 850 cc (maximum bore cut from 81 mm to 75 mm). Ride-height and holeshot devices banned, aerodynamics reduced, and 100% sustainable fuel required |

## The bikes

MotoGP bikes are prototypes. Unlike World Superbike machines, they are not based on road bikes and cannot be bought by the public.

- **Engines:** four-stroke, up to four cylinders, producing well over 250 hp.
- **Top speeds:** the official record is 368.6 km/h, set by Jorge Martín (Aprilia) at Mugello in 2026.
- **Electronics:** a standard ECU and software are shared by all teams.
- **Tyres:** a single supplier provides tyres to every team.

## Race weekend format

<Steps>
  <Step title="Friday: practice">
    Free practice and a timed practice session. The fastest riders go straight through to the second qualifying session.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Saturday: qualifying and Sprint">
    Two qualifying sessions set the grid. The Sprint, introduced in 2023, is a race about half the length of the main race and awards half points.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sunday: Grand Prix">
    The full-length main race, which awards 25 points for a win.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Legends of the premier class

- **Giacomo Agostini:** 8 premier-class titles, the most in history.
- **Valentino Rossi:** 7 premier-class titles across the 500 cc and MotoGP eras.
- **Marc Márquez:** 7 premier-class titles, the latest in 2025.
